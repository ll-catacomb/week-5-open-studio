# Notebooks, research, and notes becoming writing

Ten independent research reports commissioned at Marlon's request on 2026-10-07, with an emphasis on history and the transformations between collecting material and making an essay or argument. The reports extend the existing [week 5 theme inspirations](../README.md).

All ten reports are complete. Each provides a substantial investigation, a comparison table, a documented or explicitly reconstructed note-to-output walkthrough, two week 5 experiments, precise source leads, and an annotated access record. The collection includes twenty report-specific experiments, with additional comparative exercises in the synthesis and methods guide. [Research and review record](research-record.md).

Start with [the synthesis](synthesis.md) for connections between reports, [the research methods](research-methods.md) for ways to investigate the subject, or [the shared brief](research-brief.md) for the questions given to all ten agents.

## A larger map of possible investigations

These are proposed research routes; inclusion here does not mean this collection has completed their histories. The ten reports give us a substantial start while leaving room for a much larger program.

| Route | Question or object to investigate | Likely way into the evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Materials and manufacture | What does paper cost, binding, size, ruling, or erasability permit? | Stationers' catalogues, patents, conservation records, actual books |
| Tools and gestures | How do pen, pencil, shorthand, typing, dictation, and recording change capture? | Instruction manuals, recorded workflows, successive versions |
| Marginalia | How does a mark in a book become an extract or an argument? | Annotated copies alongside notebooks and drafts |
| Memory and rehearsal | Are notes prompts for recall, substitutes for memory, or occasions for practice? | Contemporary advice compared with actual entries |
| Excerpting and commonplacing | What happens when a passage is removed from its original context? | Source passages, headings, excerpt collections, reuse |
| Classification | Who chooses the categories, and what cannot fit them? | Indexes, revisions of headings, uncategorized entries |
| Cross-referencing | How does a later reader find or connect an earlier note? | Page references, marginal pointers, card links, digital links |
| Visual thinking | What can a sketch, diagram, map, chart, or layout do that prose cannot? | Working drawings and the outputs they inform |
| Scientific work | How are observations, anomalies, and failed trials retained or rewritten? | Laboratory records, correspondence, draft papers |
| Mathematical work | What distinguishes scratch calculation, proof attempt, and publishable demonstration? | Working sheets and successive proofs |
| Literary composition | How do quotations, scenes, lists, and observations become prose? | Genetic editions and complete writing dossiers |
| Essay traditions | How does a writer turn a collection into a claim, counterclaim, or sequence of reflection? | Notes matched to particular essay passages |
| Domestic knowledge | How do recipe and remedy books accumulate and transmit practice? | Multiple hands, additions, provenance, household records |
| Administrative and commercial records | When do lists, ledgers, and minutes become decisions or reports? | Institutional archives and successive summaries |
| Student notebooks | What is copied, reconstructed, displayed, tested, or later reused? | Exercise books, curricula, teacher instructions, assessment |
| Field research | How is observed experience transformed into credible evidence? | Jottings, expanded notes, analytic memos, published accounts |
| Journalism and interviewing | What survives from conversation into quotation and narrative? | Interview recordings, notebooks, fact-checking files |
| Theatre and production | How does research become a rehearsal choice or production? | Dossiers, sketchbooks, promptbooks, rehearsal annotations |
| Collective notebooks | Who can add, revise, retrieve, or contest shared knowledge? | Different hands, meeting records, permissions, version histories |
| Intellectual labor | Who reads aloud, transcribes, copies, indexes, edits, or types for the named author? | Correspondence, wages, acknowledgments, handwriting evidence |
| Gender and access | Whose collections can survive and be recognized as intellectual work? | Household archives, institutional access, catalogue categories |
| Disability and access | Which practices make recording and revisiting knowledge possible for particular users? | First-person accounts, assistive tools, instructional adaptations |
| Colonial collecting | How do encounters, translation, coercion, and classification shape the record? | Field archives read with sources from represented communities |
| Oral and Indigenous knowledge | When is a notebook helpful, intrusive, inappropriate, or insufficient? | Community-specific scholarship and protocols; no universal model |
| Identity and consumption | What does acquiring a notebook promise about the person its user will become? | Advertising, diaries, design, consumer accounts |
| Accountability | When does a notebook become proof, property, audit trail, or legal evidence? | Lab policies, institutional rules, disputes and adjudicated cases |
| Failure and incompletion | What do abandoned systems and unused notes tell us? | Unfinished books, discarded drafts, complaints, unused headings |
| Archival survival | Why are some notes saved, erased, recopied, edited, or digitized? | Provenance, cataloguing history, editorial omissions |
| Digital organization | What do search, folders, tags, hyperlinks, and citation managers change? | Historical software documentation and observed use |
| Computational notebooks | How are code, data, explanation, and execution made inspectable? | Versioned notebooks and reproducibility studies |
| AI-mediated notes | Who selects and paraphrases, and how can claims be traced back? | Source-grounded trials with captured outputs and corrections |

## Evidence and authorship

All ten reports are agent-authored research, not Marlon's notes. They link sources near claims, distinguish documented transformations from reconstructed demonstrations, and record access limits. Bibliographies include further research leads where full sources were unavailable. A catalogue record verifies an object's description; it does not automatically verify every proposed claim about how that object was used.
