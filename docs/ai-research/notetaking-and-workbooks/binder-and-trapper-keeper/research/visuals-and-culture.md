### 1. Wyant's patent drawings, US 3,870,223 (1975), public domain

- Source page: https://patents.google.com/patent/US3870223A/en (PDF: https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/e2/e6/c3/4909976951f172/US3870223.pdf)

### 3. Soennecken's 1886 patent, DRP 40065, public domain but not downloaded

- Correction for the essay: the 1886 Soennecken patent most often cited is **Patentschrift Nr. 40065, "Papier-Locher für Sammelmappen, Briefordner u. dergl."** (paper punch for binders, letter files, and the like), granted from November 14, 1886. It is a **hole punch** patent. The common claim that Soennecken "invented the ring binder in 1886" rests on secondary sources; I found no ring-binder patent drawing.
- Source pages: Google Patents record https://patents.google.com/patent/DE40065C/de (no drawing images served); DPMA register https://register.dpma.de/DPMAregister/pat/PatSchrifteneinsicht?docId=DE40065C (requires a browser session; curl got "Authentication required"); DEPATISnet document DE000000040065A.
- A clean reproduction of the patent drawing appears on the German Patent and Trade Mark Office's poster "Papierlocher, Friedrich Soennecken, Bonn 1886": https://www.dpma.de/docs/postergalerieneu/07_papierlocher.pdf (poster © DPMA 2023; the 1886 drawing itself is public domain by age, but the poster's layout is DPMA's). Link only; if Marlon wants the drawing on the wall, pull the page from DEPATISnet in a browser.
- Caption (if used): "Soennecken's 1886 patent for a two-pin punch 'for binders and letter files,' the tool that made the punched sheet, and so the ring binder, standard."
- Public-domain stand-ins downloaded: `assets/commons-friedrich-soennecken-portrait-c1880-PD.jpg` (portrait, c. 1880, Stadtarchiv Bonn, public domain, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Friedrich_Soennecken_1848-1919.jpg) and `assets/commons-soennecken-hole-punch-early-1900s-greg09091955-CC-BY-SA-3.0.jpg` (a Soennecken punch kept in the uploader's family "since the early 20th century," photographed January 27, 2013 by Greg09091955, CC BY-SA 3.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Soennecken_hole_punch.jpg). The punch's date is the uploader's family account, unverified.

## B. School supplies as taste, status, belonging, and a new self

### The fresh start

- Hengchen Dai, Katherine L. Milkman, and Jason Riis, "The Fresh Start Effect: Temporal Landmarks Motivate Aspirational Behavior," *Management Science* 60, no. 10 (2014): 2563–2582, https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901 (author PDF: https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Dai_Fresh_Start_2014_Mgmt_Sci.pdf). Three field studies (Google searches for "diet," university gym visits, stickK goal commitments) show aspirational behavior rising after temporal landmarks, explicitly including "the beginning of a new week, month, year, and school semester." Their mechanism: landmarks open "new mental accounting periods" that "relegate past imperfections to a previous period." The paper does not study school supplies; the bridge to the new binder as the vehicle of a new self is the essay's, not the authors'.

### Children, goods, and belonging (scholarship)

- Allison J. Pugh, *Longing and Belonging: Parents, Children, and Consumer Culture* (University of California Press, 2009). Fieldwork with affluent and low-income families in Oakland, California; children trade in an "economy of dignity," where owning or being able to talk about the right goods buys a voice among peers. The single best frame for the essay's "belonging" section. Won the ASA Children and Youth section's Distinguished Contribution award.
- Richard Elliott and Clare Leonard, "Peer pressure and poverty: exploring fashion brands and consumption symbolism among children of the 'British poor,'" *Journal of Consumer Behaviour* 3, no. 4 (2004): 347–359, https://doi.org/10.1002/cb.147. Children aged 8–12 in low-income UK families judged other children by their brands and believed branded goods would protect them from being bullied.
- Stuart Roper and Binita Shah, "Vulnerable consumers: the social impact of branding on children," *Equal Opportunities International* 26, no. 7 (2007): 712–728, https://doi.org/10.1108/02610150710822339. Low-income children use brands to avoid stigma, at real cost to their families.
- Daniel Thomas Cook, *The Commodification of Childhood: The Children's Clothing Industry and the Rise of the Child Consumer* (Duke University Press, 2004), https://www.dukeupress.edu/the-commodification-of-childhood. How merchants learned in the twentieth century to address the child, not the mother.
- Juliet B. Schor, *Born to Buy: The Commercialized Child and the New Consumer Culture* (Scribner, 2004). Marketing to children shapes "who they think they are."
- João Ewerton da Silva Santos, "Entre o desejo e a necessidade: como crianças influenciam as compras de material escolar no período de volta às aulas," undergraduate thesis, Universidade Federal da Paraíba, 2025, https://repositorio.ufpb.br/jspui/handle/123456789/38449. The one study found that is about back-to-school supply shopping as such: children influence purchases, parents keep the final say. Student work; cite lightly.

### The Trapper Keeper as identity (journalism and first-person accounts, all retrospective)

- Shawn Robare, "Of Trapper Keepers and childhood identity…," *Plastic Rocket Pop!*, May 22, 2012, https://plasticrocketpop.com/6240/of-trapper-keepers-and-childhood-identity. The fullest first-person account found. Summer 1987, age 10, entering fifth grade: he wanted one for years, his mother said no, he staged a broken-wrist argument at a Woolworth's and chose the orange, green, and purple Lamborghini cover. "These binders became the equivalent of how we use internet avatars these days. You picked the folder that best represented 'you'. Heck, I can distinctly remember referring to other students who I didn't know by what their Trapper Keeper looked like." Written 25 years after the fact.
- NYT obituary of E. Bryant Crutchfield, August 25, 2022, https://www.nytimes.com/2022/08/25/us/e-bryant-crutchfield-dead.html: "The Trapper Keeper conferred personality, and invited further customization." Secondary reporting (the Best Life and KPEL listicles) attributes to the NYT a Mead claim that by the end of the 1980s half of all middle and high school students owned one; unverified, not located in the obituary text.
- NPR, *All Things Considered*, "Remembering the man behind the Trapper Keeper," August 30, 2022 (Ailsa Chang with Erin McCarthy of *Mental Floss* and Ken Crutchfield), transcript https://www.npr.org/transcripts/1120126860. "More than 75 million" sold.
- *The Natchez Democrat* (Natchez, Mississippi), "School supplies make the child, break the parent," Staff Reports, September 17, 2006, dek: "The Trapper Keeper is the single best school supply ever invented." https://www.natchezdemocrat.com/news/school-supplies-make-the-child-break-the-parent-20406/ (paywalled; only the headline and dek were read).

### Cost and access

- **Then:** Eastland advertisement, August 7, 1983, a $2.99 ring binder beside a $4.77 Trapper Keeper (in `assets/brittain-eastland-19830807.png`, documented in essay.md). That is a 60 percent premium for the brand and the system, per child, per year, before the folders wore out.
- **Then, as remembered:** Robare's 1987 account turns on a parent saying no (his sister had one; "my mom didn't think I'd need it").
- **A specific place and date, now:** Brockport Central School District (Monroe County, New York), 2023: the district dropped class supply lists and buys supplies for every student; 45 percent of students qualify for free or reduced-price lunch; the superintendent's figure is about $70 for a fifth grader's list bought by a parent versus about $25 bought by the district; "We want to make sure it's equitable for all students." Hailie Higgins, News10NBC (WHEC), https://www.whec.com/?p=326422 (exact publication day not confirmed).
- **National picture:** Mark Lieberman, "Every Student Needs School Supplies. But Who Pays for Them?" *Education Week*, September 6, 2023, https://www.edweek.org/leadership/every-student-needs-school-supplies-but-who-pays-for-them/2023/09.
