# How research enters the rehearsal room

Research becomes theatrical when somebody can do something with it. A photograph changes a posture. A recording becomes a musical pattern. A correction travels from the rehearsal room to the people making the production.

This exhibition follows two examples: Mother Courage, whose staging became the Courage Model Book, and London Road, whose interview recordings became performance material. Their workbooks contain different kinds of knowledge: evidence, interpretation, practical instructions, and records of decisions already made.

Look at the arrows between them. Some connections are documented in dated notes. Others depend on later recollections. Some remain uncertain. A photograph of a performance cannot itself prove what inspired it.

A shared dossier can invite experiments or make one interpretation seem compulsory. Who collected the material? Who selected it? Who can disagree? Preparation becomes a creative resource when its sources, revisions, contributors, and unanswered questions remain visible to everyone using it in the room.
