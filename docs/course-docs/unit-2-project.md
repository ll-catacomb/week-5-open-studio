# Unit 2 Project: Choose a Form and Learn to Make It

Choose a form of media you would like to learn to make. Study examples, find out how they work, practice the skills they require, and make a piece of your own. Across October, our weekly activities will take you through that process: collecting references, researching, practicing, producing, editing, and talking about your work.

**TDM 155AI · Unit 2: October · First Project assignment draft · Final package due Friday, November 6, 2026**

Your form might be a cooking video, a photo essay, a comedy sketch, a short documentary, an audio story, a music video, or another form that interests you. Be specific enough to find examples and identify things you can practice. “Sports” is a subject; a match recap, an instructional video, and a sports interview are different forms.

**You will use AI to help you study your form and learn how to make it. You do not have to use AI to generate the finished work.** You might use AI to find references, explain a technique, help design a practice exercise, or troubleshoot an editing problem, then make your piece with a camera, microphone, musical instrument, drawing materials, or other conventional tools. Generative media is also an option.

## Week 4: Collect things you want to make

**September 30 · The Forms of Content**

Start these two activities in class and continue both outside class this week: Remix a Meme and Make a Media Inspiration Board (on the week 4 site).

Start a playlist, scrapbook, Pinterest board, or annotated list of examples that make you think, “I’d like to learn to do that.” You can explore more than one possible direction at this stage. Share discoveries in Slack and say what draws you to them: a way of framing a shot, a structure, a performance, a rhythm, a joke.

Try a couple of small imitations or remixes, beginning with memes in class. Notice what you preserve from the original form and what happens when you change something. What do you get from working within an established form? Where might you want to depart from it?

## Week 5: Choose your direction and build your notebook

**October 7 · Research**

Turn your collection into a repository for learning and practicing whatever you want to make, whether it's a music video, a documentary, or a website. The Notebook is that repository: a resource you can actually work from, in a form that both another person and an AI assistant can read. Use AI to help research the form, its conventions, the world or subject of your piece, and the techniques you'll need. Bring together:

- Examples of the form, with your observations about how they work and what you want to learn from them.
- Research into the subject or world of your piece.
- Useful sources, tutorials, and explanations of techniques you'll need.
- A description of what you intend to make, the skills you need to develop, and questions you still need to answer.

Each has a place here:

- **[Notebook](/notebook)**: your own notes, observations, and plans. [Dictate](/dictate) them or [photograph](/transcribe) pages you've written by hand.
- **[How-to](../how-to/README.md)**: Claude's ideas on how to make your project, kept so you can test them.
- **[AI research](../ai-research/README.md)**: research written with AI about your form and its world.
- **[Readings](../readings/README.md)**: the reading you still need to do.

The materials should suit your form: video clips, recordings, images, scripts, diagrams, or other useful references. Follow references back to their sources and check what the AI tells you. By this stage, commit to a form and a manageable piece.

**Today's task: start your how-tos.** Ask Claude to think about what you want to make and write tutorials for you, saved as Markdown in [`docs/how-to/`](../how-to/README.md), one per technique. If you're making a music video, ask it how music videos are planned, shot, and edited, and to write how-to docs for the parts you'll need to learn. For example:

> I'm making a music video for a song I recorded with friends. Think about what I'll need to learn to make it, then write a few how-to docs in `docs/how-to/`, one per technique, each with steps I can practice.

Read what it writes against your examples. These are the starting points for next week's studies.

## Week 6: Practice the small moves

**October 14 · Sketches, Studies, and Learning**

Break your project into skills you can rehearse. If you’re making a cooking video, you might practice overhead framing, recording the sound of a knife chopping, or cutting between stages of a recipe.

Make repeated small studies, compare attempts, and use AI to help you understand problems and plan your next try. Keep examples that show what changed. Be ready to explain what you can now notice or do that you couldn’t before. Begin producing the material you’ll need for editing.

## Week 7: Assemble and edit your piece

**October 21 · Editing**

Bring material you can work with. Select, cut, arrange, revise, and refine it into a whole. Return to your references: which conventions are helping you, and where do you want to depart from them?

Keep track of significant decisions so you can explain how the piece developed. Work toward a piece you can show and discuss in the following week’s interview.

## Week 8: Publicize your piece on the podcast

**October 28 · Communication and Interviewing (the Podcast Week)**

This is where your piece goes public: you talk about it on a podcast. You’ll take both roles: talking with a peer about your work and interviewing someone about theirs. Prepare by studying the work and its context. As a guest, explain your intentions, your process, what you learned, and the choices you made. As a host, ask questions that help someone else communicate what matters about their project.

We’ll record these conversations, then use our editing skills to prepare the interviews for the final package.

## Keep showing your work

Share your weekly activity and oral reflection through the course’s usual Slack routine. These activities build toward your project: the examples become your notebook, the notebook informs your studies, and the studies prepare you to make and edit the piece.
