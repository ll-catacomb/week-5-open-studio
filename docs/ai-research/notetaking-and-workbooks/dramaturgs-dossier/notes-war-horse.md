# Research notes: *War Horse* (National Theatre, 2007)

## Why this production

The NT Archive's production record PERF6860 links about 60 catalogued items, several of them research and rehearsal material described item by item: a stage-management "SM Bible" with research sections, the designer's sketchbooks, the production office's research-image file, script drafts, education packs, and rehearsal photographs. Several research-to-staging links are stated in the creators' own published words. https://catalogue.nationaltheatre.org.uk/CalmView/Record.aspx?src=CalmView.Performance&id=PERF6860

**Runner-up: *Oh What a Lovely War* (Theatre Workshop, 1963).** The better story of hidden research labour and disputed credit: Gerry Raffles heard Charles Chilton's BBC radio feature *The Long Long Trail* on Armistice Day 1962; Littlewood took it up only after refusing military uniforms, which led to the pierrot costumes; the production used scenes from Alan Clark's *The Donkeys* "initially without acknowledgement: Clark took Littlewood to court to get credited" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oh,_What_a_Lovely_War!). But the British Library acquired the Theatre Royal Stratford East / Theatre Workshop archive (140 boxes, including "Joan Littlewood's cast notes for performances of *Oh, What a Lovely War!* (1963-1964)") with cataloguing due to "begin in 2021" (https://stratfordeast.com/news/a-new-home-for-the-stratford-east-archive-at-the-british-library); no item-level records could be verified.

## 1. What the NT Archive holds

**Access.** Free and open to all by appointment, Monday–Thursday, 10am–1pm and 2–5pm, at the NT Studio, 83–101 The Cut, London SE1 8LL; book through the online form or archive@nationaltheatre.org.uk (https://www.nationaltheatre.org.uk/about-us/archive/). Only catalogue descriptions are online; none of the SM, PO, or LI files below is digitised. Records show "© National Theatre"; reproduction needs permission from the NT and, for drawings and photographs, from Rae Smith and Simon Annand. The catalogue search page returned "database may be down"; direct record URLs work, pattern `https://catalogue.nationaltheatre.org.uk/CalmView/Record.aspx?src=CalmView.Catalog&id=` + reference with `/` as `%2f`.

## 2. Kinds of document

- **Research dossier:** no single one. Research is spread across the SM Bible's "Research" sections, the production office's research images (PO/3/1/148a), and group research in rehearsal. Findlay: "a Cornish farmer's diary," "the exact pattern of First World War bugle calls," "most of our research came from the Imperial War Museum, though we also made use of local county councils… and even first-hand information from various cast members' grandparents"; a serving sergeant drilled the cast in Rehearsal Room One (2011 pack pp. 15–16, https://images.nationaltheatre.org.uk/uploads/2023/10/2011_War-Horse-Education-Pack.pdf).
- **Designer's reference and sketchbook:** Rae Smith's "Sketch Book Notes" and drawing files (573e) are both research and product: projected on stage, and a sketchbook made into a prop.
- **Prompt book, plots, reports:** DSM prompt copy and ASM plots (573c–d), plus show reports, read by the team running the show.
- **Script drafts:** rehearsals began on draft nine and reached "something like draft 12 by Press Night" (Findlay, 2011 pack p. 15). Only the 2006 and 2010 drafts are archived.
- **Made afterward (not the original research pack):** the 2011 education pack, "created to accompany our tour… in 2011," edited by Gosden and Clare with Dawn Ingleson credited (https://www.nationaltheatre.org.uk/learn-explore/resources/war-horse-education-resource/), reprinting Findlay's 2007 account and the Puppet rules and adding 2010–11 cast interviews; the 2024 tour pack by Susie Ferguson (https://images.nationaltheatre.org.uk/uploads/2023/10/2024_War-Horse_Education-Pack.pdf); Mervyn Millar, *The Horse's Mouth* (NT/Oberon 2007, updated 2011), by the production's Associate and a puppeteer (https://www.britishtheatreguide.info/reviews/the-horse-s-mou-13432); the *Making War Horse* DVD (2009).

## 3. Source → staging chains

- **A. Watching horses → Joey's breathing and ears (strongest).** Adrian Kohler, *Ingenia*, September 2009 (https://ingenia.org.uk/articles/engineering-movement/): "while observing real horses breathing hard after physical exertion, the problem of representing this looked daunting." Ribs move outward, invisible in profile; the front-leg axle moves in a slot so the "heart" puppeteer can "make the rib cage go up and down simply by bending and straightening their knees… The up-and-down replaces the more accurate side-to-side, but it is more effective because you can see it." Ears needed 180° because "pointing forward they show interest, pointing backwards they indicate fear"; cable-wound axles were his "Eureka" moment. "The design of the tail movement also lay in understanding the anatomy of a real horse." Workshop testing forced a third, outside puppeteer for the head.
- **B. Horse research → "Puppet rules" → SM Bible and education pack.** Findlay: the Joey and Topthorn teams drew up "lists of characteristics and tendencies" "displayed in the room," plus a list for actors working with the horses, e.g. "Horses don't like it if you look them in the eye…" (2011 pack pp. 17–18). The same rules are in 573a. Later casts: "two weeks purely of horse puppetry technique and horse study… videos of Monty Roberts and foal research on YouTube" (Sarah Mardel, pp. 23–24); Al Nedjari on Monty Roberts "training" the puppets and a night with the King's Troop, Royal Horse Artillery (pp. 25–26). **Uncertain:** Nedjari joined for the 2008 revival.
- **C. Rae Smith's sketchbook → torn-paper screen and Nicholls.** Smith: the strips "came about almost by accident when she tried using a little of her sketchbook to make an illustration to be projected on screen. 'And that… is how Captain Nicholls developed, as an amateur artist whose war landscapes could be used in a moving and purposeful way'" (2011 pack p. 19). Mark Grimmer: "The concept for the video world was that it was to feature drawings from the sketchbook of one of the characters, Lieutenant Nicholls," with "bespoke film footage, some archival material" (https://www.livedesignonline.com/theatre/war-horse-s-animal-magnetism-part-3, 2011); style shifts toward "the more aggressive and abstract Vorticism used by the war artists" (*LSI*, January 2008, https://www.theatrecrafts.com/archive/documents/lsi_jan2008_warhorse.pdf); Grimmer names Paul Nash. Caveats: Nicholls already sketches Joey in the novel; a Shropshire Star piece dating Smith's Ellesmere drawing trip to about 2011 conflicts with 2007 (https://www.shropshirestar.com/whats-on/arts-and-entertainment/shropshires-key-role-in-worldwide-war-horse-production-with-video-6156901). The sketchbook prop in 573e is the book Albert tears Joey's picture from.
- **D. Dated diary sketches → dates on the projections.** "the sketches in my grandfather's notebook are dated like that… so there's a level of authenticity," also marking "the passage of time" (2024 pack p. 11). **Uncertain** whether the speaker is Morris or the joint Morris/Katie Henry section; most likely Morris. Told in 2024 about 2007.
- **E. Hymnbook → the Song Man.** John Tams: "I trawled obsessively, the Moody and Sankey hymnbook seeking songs" (https://klofmag.com/2017/10/garland-for-joey-war-horse-songbook). "Only Remembered": Horatius Bonar's text, Ira Sankey's setting, with "additional and adapted verses" by Tams (https://mainlynorfolk.info/coope.boyes.simpson/songs/onlyremembered.html). The Original and Final Song Books (573b) suggest revision in rehearsal; confirm in person.
- **F. Not established: the 1916–17 letters and WWI service records (573b).** The catalogue does not say what they shaped. Nearest link: "Ideas for Joey's Night Scene 27"; Grimmer describes that sequence broken into "several shorter cues which dovetailed together" (Live Design). A reading-room lead only.

## 5. Where the research hardened

- Puppet rules → SM Bible → education handout; Mardel (2010–11): "ground rules are set up and taught to all actors in the beginning," with "puppetry associates on hand to tell us if we were acting 'out of character' for a real horse."
- Transfers: West End 2009–2016 (3,000+ performances), Broadway 2011–13, Berlin, Amsterdam, Beijing (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Horse_(play)); the archive keeps the 2012 Australian tour drawings beside the 2007 ones (573e).
- Revivals re-research: Morris in 2024, "we're not simply mechanically putting it back together"; the cast read *All Quiet on the Western Front*, visited the Household Cavalry, heard Tams; a drama therapist joined the room (2024 pack p. 10). But also (p. 11, speaker uncertain): "This is a revival… we've got a blueprint."
- For New York the shadow puppetry was "replaced with animation" (Grimmer, Live Design). The Faber playtext (2007, ISBN 9780571240159) fixed one moment's script.
