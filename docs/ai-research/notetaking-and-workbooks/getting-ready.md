# Getting ready

Week 5 is about the stage before production: "research, preparation, organizing systems, toolsets" (Marlon, 2026-10-04). These are examples of that stage from other parts of life, to think with.

## Marlon's examples (2026-10-04)

- "getting your school supplies together in 8th grade"
- "buying your textbooks and getting your materials in your first year of college"

What these two have in common, as reference points:

- **The supply list.** Schools and teachers hand out a list (binders, a particular calculator, colored pencils, a planner), so getting ready starts from someone else's idea of what the work will need. Families shop from it in late August, and the store sets out the aisle.
- **Setting up the binder.** Tabs per subject, a planner filled in with the schedule, labels written on everything. The organizing happens before there's anything to organize; the system anticipates the work.
- **The first year of college.** The syllabus lists the books, the bookstore shelves them by course, and there's a choice of new, used, rented, or found somewhere else. Course packs and readers collect what the instructor assembled. At Harvard this used to coincide with shopping week, when students sat in on courses before choosing them.
- **Moving in.** The dorm room is set up before classes start: the desk, the lamp, where the books go. A space arranged for the work to come.

## Kitchens, production, and the stage

- **Mise en place.** French for "putting in place": before service, each cook in a professional kitchen preps and arranges every ingredient and tool at their station, so that during service nothing is fetched or chopped. Prep lists drive it, and the station is laid out the same way every day.
- **Pre-production.** In film and TV, the stage between development (research, rights, script) and the shoot: casting, locations, the shot list, the schedule, the budget, hiring crew, assembling and testing kit. See [`project-models.md`](project-models.md).
- **The series bible and the dramaturg's casebook.** A TV writers' room works from a bible (characters, world, rules, tone); a theatre production's dramaturg assembles a casebook of research (history, sources, images, earlier productions) for the director and cast before rehearsal.
- **Load-in and tech week.** In theatre, the set, lights, and sound go into the venue and are tested before the performers arrive, then cued through in technical rehearsals. The prop table is laid out and labeled so each prop is always in the same place.

## Tools and craft

- **Sharpening.** Woodworkers sharpen and tune their tools before a project and come back to it as the work goes on. A saying often attributed to Lincoln, though apocryphal, has a man spending four of his six hours sharpening the axe.
- **Jigs and fixtures.** A woodworker or machinist builds a jig, a guide that holds the work or steers the tool, so a cut can be repeated exactly. Building the jig is a project of its own that makes the later work fast and consistent.
- **The painter's preparation.** Stretching and priming canvases, grinding or mixing colors, setting out the palette in a fixed order.
- **Musicians.** Tuning, setting up the instrument and gear, warming up, and learning the charts before a session.
- **The developer's environment.** Setting up a new machine: the editor, the shell, dotfiles, accounts, keys. Many developers keep their setup in a repo so a new machine can be ready in an hour.

## Expeditions and checklists

- **The packing list and the shakedown.** Expeditions and long hikes start with a gear list, then a shakedown trip to test the gear and cut what isn't needed before the real trip.
- **The pre-flight checklist.** Pilots run a written checklist before every flight, however experienced they are; surgical teams adopted the same idea (Atul Gawande, *The Checklist Manifesto*, 2009).
- **The go-bag.** A bag packed in advance so that leaving takes no preparation at the moment of leaving. Journalists, emergency workers, and new parents keep versions of it.

## Scholars and writers

- **The research file.** Newspapers kept a clippings library, "the morgue," that reporters drew on before writing; writers keep files of notes and sources for a book before drafting.
- **The commonplace book and index cards.** Readers copied passages into a commonplace book, organized by heading, to draw on later. Some novelists drafted on index cards they could reorder.
- **The literature review and the reading list.** Before doctoral research, a student reads through a field from a list (for general exams) and writes a literature review that positions the project.
- **The lab notebook.** Set up before the experiment: protocols, materials, what will be recorded.

## When getting ready becomes the work

- **Yak shaving.** Programmers' term for a chain of preparatory tasks, each needed for the one before, that ends far from the original goal.
- **Setup as avoidance.** Buying notebooks, reorganizing the system, or trying a new app can stand in for starting. Any week about preparation has this risk built in.
