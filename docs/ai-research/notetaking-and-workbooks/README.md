# Notetaking and workbooks

A collection for TDM 155AI week 5: Research. These readings investigate how people capture material, organize it, return to it, and turn it into writing, arguments, or productions.

## Start here

- [Synthesis: how notes become writing](notebooks-research/synthesis.md): connections across ten research reports.
- [The ten-report collection](notebooks-research/README.md): ancient, medieval, East Asian, commonplace, scientific, literary, card-index, fieldwork, teaching, and digital/AI practices.
- [Research methods](notebooks-research/research-methods.md): how to investigate a notetaking practice and trace evidence from source to output.

## Historical examples

- [Before the first mark](getting-ready-to-work/README.md): getting ready to work.
- [Stored thinking](stored-thinking/README.md): keeping material for future use.
- [Ancient notebooks](ancient-notebooks/README.md): reading, excerpting, and composition.
- [A handful of flowers](manipulus-florum/README.md): medieval compilation.

- [Luhmann's Zettelkasten](luhmann-zettelkasten/README.md): slips, links, and new connections.
- [Binders and Trapper Keepers](binder-and-trapper-keeper/README.md): loose-leaf organization, design, and school supplies.
- [Theatrical research](theatrical-research/README.md): research becoming production choices.
- [The dramaturg's dossier](dramaturgs-dossier/README.md): model books, sketchbooks, and rehearsal records.

The collection also includes [preparation before the work](getting-ready.md) and [ten deep research prompts](deep-research-prompts.md).

## Try this with your own project

Choose two systems and compare how each captures, sorts, retrieves, and transforms material. Then use one source to make a note and turn that note into a paragraph, diagram, or production choice. Keep the source, note, and result together so someone else can trace what changed.
