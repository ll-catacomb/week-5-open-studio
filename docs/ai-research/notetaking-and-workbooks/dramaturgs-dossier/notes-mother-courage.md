# Research notes: *Mother Courage* and the *Couragemodell 1949*

The richest open-access source is Kristopher Imbrigotta's dissertation, *Framing Brecht: Photography and Experiment in the Modellbücher* (UW–Madison, 2013), read in full: https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/R66FDNTW53CML8X/R/file-eaf98.pdf (cited as "Imbrigotta" with page numbers). BFA = the Berlin and Frankfurt edition of Brecht's works (also cited as GBA).

## 0. The A.R.T. piece, "A Model of Courage"

- https://americanrepertorytheater.org/media/a-model-of-courage/ — by Gideon Lester, then A.R.T. Resident Dramaturg, dated 9 February 2001, the opening of A.R.T.'s *Mother Courage & Her Children* at the Loeb Drama Center (director János Szász, translation Lester, music Nicholas Mustelin, set Csaba Antal, Courage: Karen MacDonald; https://americanrepertorytheater.org/shows-events/mother-courage-her-children/).
- Argument: Weigel's 1949 performance became "definitive"; Brecht turned it into the model book, a "meticulous photographic record" with notes, which with the 1960 film made Weigel's Courage "a cultural icon to be imitated or subverted by all subsequent productions."
- Reports: Brecht first wrote the part for Naima Wifstrand and the mute Kattrin for Weigel, who spoke no Swedish; after Zurich critics found Giehse's Courage sentimental he rewrote; a 1945 letter to Suhrkamp said theatres staging it "should arrange for a guest production by Weigel"; in scene 12 Weigel counts the burial money and puts one coin back, a gesture that "destroyed any sympathy."
- Brecht quotations as Lester gives them: "The Model must not be pressed too far … The aim must be neither to copy the pattern exactly nor to break away from it at once"; the model is meant "not to render thought unnecessary but to provoke it: not as a substitute for artistic creation but as its stimulus." Edition not given (they read like Willett's *Brecht on Theatre*, 1964).
- Later productions surveyed: Littlewood 1955 (Tynan: "as if Wagner were to be staged in a school gymnasium"), Dench at the RSC, Rigg at the NT 1995, Glenda Jackson in Glasgow, Bancroft on Broadway 1963, Schechner 1975, Shange 1980, Linda Hunt in Boston 1984.
- Ends: "the spirit of Helene Weigel will be stalking our rehearsal hall."

## 1. Chronology

- **Zurich, 19 April 1941**, Schauspielhaus Zürich: director Leopold Lindtberg, design Teo Otto, music mostly Paul Burkhard, Courage: Therese Giehse; about ten performances; Brecht did not see it. https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutter_Courage_und_ihre_Kinder, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_Courage_and_Her_Children, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teo_Otto
- **Berlin, Deutsches Theater, 11 January 1949**: Brecht and Erich Engel directing, Weigel as Courage, new score by Paul Dessau. https://sammlung-online.stadtmuseum.de/Details/Index/171315
  - **CONFLICT (designer), resolved as both:** Stadtmuseum Berlin's stage model BBM 90/14 credits Heinrich Kilger and Teo Otto, with the wagon turning "endlessly on the revolving stage"; the DHM also credits both (https://www.dhm.de/archiv/ausstellungen/erf/raum/erstesog/muttersprache/bild134.htm); a 1949 review speaks of "der beinahe kahlen Bühne Heinrich Kilgers … vor dem nackten Rundhorizont" (https://tdz.de/artikel/1949-mutter-courage-erstauffuehrung); Christa Hasche, *Modern Drama* 42.2 (1999): "Teo Otto's stage design was taken over for the Berlin staging, although it underwent significant modifications" (https://go.gale.com/ps/i.do?id=GALE%7CA58575764&sid=googlescholar&v=2.1&it=r). Suggested wording: Kilger's realization of Otto's Zurich design.
  - Completed before the Berliner Ensemble was formally founded (Barnett via Remshardt, https://www.umass.edu/defa/sites/default/files/Mother%20Courage%20and%20Her%20Children%20on%20Stage%20and%20Screen_by%20Ralf%20Remshardt/index.pdf).
  - "The Song of the Hours" cut for 1949, restored 1951 (Gelber, https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-20394-7_2, n. 32). Dessau's score first used by the Zurich company on tour in Vienna 1946 (same, n. 19; secondary only).
- **Munich Kammerspiele, 8 October 1950**: Brecht directing, Giehse, Otto; worked from the 1949 Berlin documentation, not the later book (Gelber, https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-20394-7_9, n. 15).
- **Berliner Ensemble revival, 11 September 1951**: 405 performances through 4 April 1961. The BE was a guest at the Deutsches Theater until it moved to the Theater am Schiffbauerdamm in March 1954; some study guides wrongly put the 1949 premiere at Schiffbauerdamm.
- **Tours and film**: Paris 1954 (first prize, Théâtre des Nations), London 1956, Paris 1957; archival recording 1957; DEFA film by Peter Palitzsch and Manfred Wekwerth shot 1960, released 1961, with Jacques Callot's Thirty Years' War etchings as scene dividers (Remshardt).

## 2. The model book

- *Couragemodell 1949*, Henschelverlag, Berlin, 1958 (after Brecht's death), series *Modellbücher des Berliner Ensemble*, three paperbound volumes: text, photographs, notes (Imbrigotta pp. 101–02). Photo volume: 106 pages of scene-by-scene shots with captions, 55 "Sequenzen," 14 "Gestisches," 3 "Beschäftigungen," 5 "Bewegte Vorgänge," about 40 variant photos from Berlin and Munich. Notes volume: 103 notes, over a tenth titled "Detail" (pp. 102, 105). Final selection and revisions 1956 (p. 102, n. 177). Catalog: https://books.google.com/books/about/Couragemodell_1949.html?id=mCErzQEACAAJ
- Pre-history (de.wikipedia only, **UNVERIFIED** elsewhere): in 1949 hectographed copies and photographs went to theatres; notes drafted by assistant director Heinz Kuckhahn and corrected by Brecht; use of the model was at first a condition of the rights, relaxed around 1954 to buying the materials; the 1958 edition prepared 1955–56 with Ruth Berlau and Peter Palitzsch.
- Partial earlier publication: *Theaterarbeit: 6 Aufführungen des Berliner Ensembles* (1952; ed. Weigel; 2nd ed. Henschel 1961), with scene timings (Gelber n. 18). 1952 publisher **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Photographers**: credit line "Szenenfotos … von Ruth Berlau, Hainer Hill und Ruth Wilhelmi" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Wilhelmi). **CONFLICT:** de.wikipedia says Berlau and Hill photographed in spring 1949, but Hill met Brecht only in 1950 and worked at the BE 1950–53 (https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hainer_Hill), so his images are probably from 1951. Imbrigotta captions the silent-scream images "© unknown (likely Hainer Hill)" (pp. 324, 336). Hill's photos are in the Bertolt-Brecht-Archiv, Akademie der Künste.
- Precursor: *Antigonemodell 1948* (Brecht and Caspar Neher, ed. Berlau, Gebrüder Weiss 1949; Henschel 1955), documenting Chur, February 1948. Revermann, "The Making of a Model," in *Brecht and Tragedy* (CUP 2021): https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/brecht-and-tragedy/making-of-a-model-antigonemodell-1948/D18637945D777998ACDAC16127DCE097; Taxidou in Felski ed., *Rethinking Tragedy* (2008).
- English: *Brecht on Performance: Messingkauf and Modelbooks*, eds. Kuhn, Giles, Silberman (Bloomsbury Methuen 2014), "Courage Model 1949" pp. 183–222, trans. Kuhn and Charlotte Ryland. Sections: Opening Remarks https://brechtguide.library.wisc.edu/catalog/03149; Details from Scene 3 https://brechtguide.library.wisc.edu/catalog/03151; Variations in Berlin and Munich https://brechtguide.library.wisc.edu/catalog/03152; Scene 12 https://brechtguide.library.wisc.edu/catalog/03153. Willett, *Brecht on Theatre* (1964): "Stage design" pp. 215–20 https://brechtguide.library.wisc.edu/catalog/00101; "Does the use of the model restrict the artist's freedom?" pp. 222–25 https://brechtguide.library.wisc.edu/catalog/00108.

### Brecht on using models

- "Man muß das Modell nicht so sehr pressen." BFA 25:172 (Imbrigotta p. 104).
- "Man muß sich frei machen von der landläufigen Verachtung des Kopierens. Es ist nicht das 'Leichtere.' Es ist nicht eine Schande, sondern eine Kunst. Das heißt, es muß zur Kunst entwickelt werden, und zwar dazu, daß keine Schablonisierung und Erstarrung eintritt." BFA 25:388 (p. 103).
- Willett, p. 224 (via Tweddle, https://studia.reviste.ubbcluj.ro/index.php/subbdramatica/article/download/4378/4117/8107): "as playwright I have copied the Japanese, Greek and Elizabethan Drama; as producer the music-hall comedian Karl Valentin's groupings and Caspar Neher's stage sketches; yet I have never felt my freedom restricted. Give me an intelligent model of King Lear, and I will find it fun to carry out."
- "Der Schöpfungsakt ist ein kollektiver Schöpfungsprozeß …" *Antigonemodell*, BFA 25:76 (p. 104).
- 1952 notice to BE staff, BFA 23:221 (p. 104): nobody seems to have understood that making the model books is an extraordinary chance to learn directing and criticism. Some at the BE called the models "Diktatur auf dem Theater."
- On detail: "Das Detail, auch das kleinste, muß … voll ausgespielt werden. Besonders gilt das für Vorgänge, die … übergangen werden, wie das Bezahlen bei einem Handel." BFA 25:185 (p. 105).
- "Sanfte Erpressung" (gentle blackmail): only on a study-aid site citing "Brecht 1964: 96". **Low confidence.**

## 3. Berlau and the assistants

- Berlau: Brecht's theatre photographer from 1947, made the photo-model books (Antigone, Courage, Galileo, Carrar), headed the BE photo archive. Simone Barck's review of Grischa Meyer, *Ruth Berlau. Fotografin an Brechts Seite* (2003), https://www.freitag.de/autoren/simone-barck/der-soziale-blick; Meyer reconstructed her work from 235 folders of contacts and negatives. She framed her work as "komplexe Dienstleistung für Brecht."
- Imbrigotta's own phrase "used Ruth Berlau as his camera eye" (p. 74) is itself an instance of the instrumental framing.
- 3 November 1954 phone call on *Antigonemodell* royalties (Berlau, *Living for Brecht*, ed. Bunge, 1987, p. 227, via Imbrigotta p. 83). Her essay "Theaterfotografie" (BFA 25:532–33) argues for proper fees.
- Weigel blacklisted her from the BE after Brecht's death (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Berlau; thinly sourced).
- Further: Bunge ed., *Brechts Lai-Tu* (1985); Sabine Kebir, *Mein Herz liegt neben der Schreibmaschine* (2006); *Brecht Yearbook* 30 (2005), *Who Was Ruth Berlau?*
- Claus Küchenmeister: "For us, of course, working on the Modelbooks was a bit of a crappy job. We sat there in a little cubbyhole, pasting things down. And whoever pasted most industriously would be in Brecht's good books." (Barnett ed., *Bertolt Brecht: Critical and Primary Sources* III, 2020, p. 245; via Gelber n. 3, https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-20394-7_9.)
- Gelber in the archive: reports "typed out … quickly on manual typewriters," "more than one assistant would report on each day's work," "dated, identified by author," photos "literally pasted into the pages of an album" (n. 47).
- Wekwerth, *Notate* (Suhrkamp 1967). Barnett, "The Rise and Fall of Modelbooks, Notate and the Brechtian Method," *TRI* 41.2 (2016), https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/theatre-research-international/article/abs/rise-and-fall-of-modelbooks-notate-and-the-brechtian-method-documentation-and-the-berliner-ensembles-changing-roles-as-a-theatre-company/DC369E9D2F6FFFB75072B22CF329BB84
- **Not verified:** that Besson, Monk, or Palitzsch kept *Courage* notebooks.
- Kenneth Tynan later produced NT "model books" for *The Recruiting Officer* (1965) and *Othello* (1967) (Gelber nn. 1, 4).

## 4. Source → staging chains

- **A. Singapore photo → silent scream (strongest).** AP photo of a woman crouching and screaming by a dead boy after the Japanese bombing of Singapore, in *Life* 23 March 1942; pasted into Brecht's *Arbeitsjournal* 5 April 1942 (BFA 27:80); reused as *Kriegsfibel* "Singapore Lament" (BFA 12:207). Model note "Beobachtung" (BFA 25:203–04): "Der Ausdruck des äußeren Schmerzes nach dem Anhören der Salve, der schreilos geöffnete Mund bei zurückgebogenem Kopf stammt vermutlich von der Pressefotografie einer indischen Frau, die während der Beschießung von Singapore bei der Leiche ihres getöteten Sohnes hockt. Die Weigel muß sie vor Jahren gesehen haben, wiewohl sie sich auf Befragen nicht daran erinnerte. So gehen Beobachtungen in den Fundus der Schauspieler ein. Die Weigel nahm diese Haltung übrigens erst in späteren Vorstellungen ein." Documented as photo sequence 119a/b–120a/b (BFA 25:317–20). Imbrigotta pp. 290–93, Fig. 12 p. 336. Caveats: "vermutlich"; Weigel did not remember; later performances only. **FOLKLORE?** No source for a Bruegel origin.
- **B. Zurich design → Berlin blocking.** Snippet of Willett's "Stage design" (*Brecht on Theatre* pp. 215–20, GBA 25:174): "We used … the famous model that Teo Otto had designed for the Zurich Theatre during the war … We maintained almost everything else … especially the excellent locations of the wagon … through it the groupings and the succession of events were to a great extent fixed from the beginning." Near-verbatim; check against the book. Hasche: the Berlin stage had no projected backdrops and was "evenly well lit in order to avoid any allusion to atmosphere."
- **C. Zurich reception → 1949 rewrites.** Critics praised "the endless vitality of the mother animal" (Hasche). Brecht: "wir haben die erste Szene der ›Courage‹ zu ändern, da hier schon angelegt ist, was bei der Zürcher Aufführung den Zuschauern erlaubt hat, sich hauptsächlich von der Dauerhaftigkeit und Tragfähigkeit der gequälten Kreatur (des ewigen Muttertiers) erschüttern zu lassen" (de.wikipedia; date UNVERIFIED; Gelber dates a related journal entry 27 October 1948). Changes in scenes 1 (Eilif lost during a sale; earlier she was reviving the sergeant, Gelber n. 16), 5 (shirts given only under coercion), 7, and 12 (Hasche). Brecht on 1949 audiences: they "did not see Mother Courage's crimes … they saw only her failure, her sufferings." Hans Mayer: the changes did not much reduce sympathy. Remshardt: Brecht "shuddered" at a Zurich critic's Niobe comparison.
- **D. Moneybag gesture.** *Brecht on Performance* (search excerpt): "Here Weigel devised (for the sale of the buckle in 1, the sale of the capon in 2, the sale of the drinks in 5 and 6, the handing out of the burial money in 12, etc.) a little gesture of her own: she audibly snapped shut the leather moneybag that she wore slung around her neck."
- **E. Revolving stage → the ending.** The long circling in scene 12 meant to make spectators think "this has gone on long enough" (Imbrigotta p. 124, BFA 25:240). Weigel yoked "like a horse"; Giehse in Munich looked into the distance and blew her nose with her finger, a variant Brecht praised (pp. 123–24). Wagon's dilapidation scene by scene: **UNVERIFIED**.
- **F. Scene 3 wagon as divider.** Note "Diesseits und Jenseits" (BFA 25:200): the wagon splits the stage, Courage trading on one side, Kattrin watching Yvette on the other (p. 289).
- **G. Film only:** Callot etchings as DEFA scene dividers (Remshardt).

## 5. Later productions and the orthodoxy debate

- **Littlewood 1955:** Theatre Workshop, Queen's Hall, Barnstaple, 30 June 1955, Devon Festival; Littlewood directed and played Courage; settings John Bury; Dessau music (https://calmview.bham.ac.uk/rdf/catalog/XMS38/2211). A UW-hosted snippet (probably Peter Thomson, *Brecht: Mother Courage and Her Children*, CUP 1997; **UNVERIFIED**): Brecht "put his foot down — either Littlewood played the part or the rights would be withdrawn"; Carl Weber, sent by Brecht, was shut out of rehearsals.
- **US estate control:** Eric Bentley interview (1996), https://itech.dickinson.edu/glossen/heft2/bentley.html — a 1959 Broadway version with Milhaud music abandoned under legal threat; Stefan Brecht set up the 1963 Crawford/Robbins/Bancroft production; Robbins "worked from the film"; photos taken every "20–30 seconds."
- **Public Theater 2006:** Kushner translation, Tesori music, George C. Wolfe, Meryl Streep; John Walter's documentary *Theater of War* (2008) sets the rehearsals beside the original (https://kinolorberedu.com/film/theaterofwar; Hilton Als, https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2006/09/04/wagon-train).
- **NT 2009:** Deborah Warner, Fiona Shaw, Kushner, Tom Pye, Duke Special; Coveney contrasted Shaw with "the gnarled old tragic heroine of the Helene Weigel or Joan Littlewood variety."
- Barnett (*TRI* 2016): documentation as a "yardstick" of BE identity, declining from the mid-1960s. Unverified snippet: the model "remained almost unquestioned for many years" until late-1960s directors needed "to establish their fundamental independence." Remshardt: Hare and Kushner push back. Bill Gelber's Texas Tech production (2015) made its own model book with smartphones and slides (https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-20394-7_9), a direct analogue for the course.
